The Three Pillars of an Unshaken Life

Three lessons I discovered in someone who never knew she was teaching me.

JJ

By Jedidiah Jsk

Jul 2026 • 8 min read

Not every teacher stands in a classroom. Some teach us simply by the way they live, often without ever knowing that someone is watching, learning, and changing because of them.

I met such a person someone the world may never know as a teacher, but whom I have come to regard as one of the greatest teachers of my life.

I found three rare qualities in her character: she was not ruled by what she did not have, not proud of what she had, and not imprisoned by what she had lost.

I first saw these qualities in her. Then I understood them. Then I learned from them. And eventually, I began making them part of my own life. (Take the good you find in people. Make it part of you.)

Some of the greatest teachers never know that they have taught us anything.

This article is my attempt to pass those lessons forward the three simple qualities that became three extraordinary pillars of an unshaken life.

1. The First Pillar - Do Not Become a Prisoner of What Is Missing

There will almost always be something we do not have. Someone has a larger house, someone earns more, someone received the opportunity we wanted, someone found love earlier, someone travelled farther, and someone appears to be living the life we once imagined for ourselves.

The human mind has a strange habit. It can stand inside a beautiful garden and spend all its time looking at the one flower growing beyond the wall.

Wanting something is not wrong. Desire has built cities, created art, discovered medicines, started businesses, and moved human beings toward better lives. The problem begins when desire stops being a direction and becomes a condition for happiness.

There is an enormous difference between thinking, “I would like to have this,” and believing, “I cannot be happy until I have this.” The first gives us direction. The second gives away our freedom.

Want something deeply, but never hand it the keys to your happiness.

We often imagine peace waiting somewhere in the future after the promotion, after the house, after the recognition, after the relationship, or after the bank balance reaches a certain number.

We keep postponing life until life satisfies our conditions.

But the horizon has an ancient trick: walk toward it, and it moves. There will always be another milestone, another comparison, another desire, another version of “enough.”

The person who learns to remain peaceful while something is still missing discovers a rare kind of freedom. Ambition remains, but desperation disappears. You can work hard without becoming restless. You can dream without becoming miserable. You can pursue tomorrow without insulting today.

2. The Second Pillar - Do Not Build a Throne From What You Have

If absence creates desire, possession creates another danger: pride.

There is nothing wrong with enjoying success or celebrating achievement. But somewhere between gratitude and superiority, the human mind can quietly lose its balance.

Money becomes status. Knowledge becomes arrogance. Beauty becomes vanity. Position becomes power over others. Achievement becomes identity. Eventually, the things we possess begin possessing us.

Gratitude says, “I am fortunate to have this.” Pride says, “I am greater because I have this.”

The difference may appear small, but it is enormous.

Think of a tree filled with fruit. The more fruit its branches carry, the lower they bend. Human beings often do the opposite the more we acquire, the higher we try to stand.

Perhaps wisdom asks us to become more like the tree. Let achievement make us useful, not arrogant. Let knowledge make us curious, not dismissive. Let wealth increase generosity, not distance. Let authority increase responsibility, not ego.

Everything we possess carries an invisible temporary label. Our titles can change, our bodies age, markets fall, companies disappear, applause moves toward someone else, and even extraordinary talent eventually meets time.

Perhaps the healthiest relationship with success is not absolute ownership, but stewardship. For some period of life, something has been placed in our hands. Use it well. Enjoy it. Share it. Protect it. But never confuse what you possess with who you are.

3. The Third Pillar - Do Not Spend the Present Living Inside the Past

The first pillar concerns what never arrived. The second concerns what is here. The third may be the hardest because it concerns what left.

It could be a person, a friendship, a job, a home, an opportunity, money, youth, status, a dream, or sometimes even an earlier version of ourselves.

Loss is unavoidable because change is unavoidable. Yet the mind has a remarkable ability to keep departed things alive. The event ends, but we replay it. The person leaves, but the conversations continue inside us. The opportunity disappears, but we construct imaginary worlds around what might have happened.

The calendar moves forward while part of us remains standing at an old door.

Memory is a place to visit, not a house in which to live.

This does not mean we should become emotionless. Grief deserves respect, and some losses change us permanently. Acceptance does not mean pretending something never mattered. In fact, grief often exists precisely because something mattered enormously.

But there comes a point when remembering something and continuing to suffer because of it become two different things.

We can honour what was without demanding that it return. A river does not negotiate with the water that has already passed. It receives what comes next. Perhaps we must learn the same wisdom.

What left us may have given us love. It may have given us pain. It may have given us knowledge. Sometimes it gave us all three.

Take the lesson. Carry the gratitude. Leave the chains.

Three Pillars, Three Relationships With Time

These three lessons may appear separate, but beneath them lies something deeper: they are three different relationships with time.

What we do not have usually belongs to an imagined future. What we have belongs to the present. What we have lost belongs to the past.

And human beings can become trapped in all three. We chase the future, glorify the present, and mourn the past. Meanwhile, life quietly continues.

The future asks for effort. The present asks for humility. The past asks for acceptance.

Perhaps this is the deeper architecture behind the three pillars: toward what has not arrived, patience; toward what has arrived, humility; toward what has departed, acceptance.

Together, they create balance.

Imagine holding something valuable in your hand. If your hand is empty, you do not have to hate the emptiness. If something beautiful is placed there, you do not have to believe it makes you greater than the person beside you. And if one day it leaves your hand, you do not have to spend the rest of your life clutching its memory.

Perhaps life asks us to hold things with an open hand-our dreams, achievements, relationships, possessions, and even our identities.

The problem begins when the open hand becomes a closed fist. We grip desire. We grip success. We grip memory. Then we wonder why living hurts.

An open hand can receive. An open hand can give. And when the time comes, an open hand can let go.

Wisdom is not having nothing. Wisdom is understanding that nothing has to remain forever for life to remain meaningful.

These principles are not against ambition. They do not ask us to stop wanting a better life, reject success, or become indifferent when something meaningful disappears.

They ask for something more difficult: freedom within ambition.

A person who does not collapse over what is missing can keep moving. A person who is not intoxicated by success can remain grounded. A person who can accept loss can begin again.

Build. Compete. Create. Earn. Love. Dream enormously.

But keep your inner life independent of the scoreboard.

When you fail, learn. When you succeed, remain grounded. When something ends, honour the chapter and turn the page.

The purpose is not detachment from life.

It is freedom within life.

The Quiet Test

Perhaps these three pillars can become a simple test we occasionally give ourselves.

Think about something you deeply want but do not yet have. Can you pursue it without allowing its absence to poison today?

Now think about something valuable you currently possess. Can you enjoy it without believing that having it makes you more important than someone who does not?

Finally, remember something meaningful that life has taken away. Can you honour what it meant without allowing its absence to consume what remains?

These are uncomfortable questions. That is why they matter.

Character is rarely revealed by what we say when everything is comfortable. It appears in our relationship with wanting, having, and losing.

A peaceful person is not someone life has spared. It is someone life has failed to enslave.

Conclusion - An Unshaken Life

Life is continuously moving things through three invisible doors.

Through one door are the things we hope will enter our lives. Through another are the things temporarily standing beside us. Through the last are the things whose time with us has ended.

We cannot control all three doors. But we can decide who we become while standing between them.

A person tortured by what is missing will always feel poor, regardless of how much they eventually acquire. A person made arrogant by what is present will eventually discover how temporary possession can be. A person imprisoned by what is gone may become so busy looking backward that they fail to see what is arriving.

There is another way.

Seek without desperation. Possess without arrogance. Lose without losing yourself.

Perhaps this is what my unknown teacher taught me without ever intending to teach me.

Not a life without ambition. Not a life without achievement. Not even a life without sorrow.

Something far more difficult:

A life in which none of them becomes your master.

Maybe that is one of the quietest definitions of freedom.

Do not measure your life only by what entered your hands. Measure it also by what never gained control of your heart.

Three pillars: do not be ruled by what is missing, do not become proud because of what is present, and do not surrender your remaining life to what has passed.

Everything else will continue to change.

But if these three pillars remain standing, perhaps we can remain standing too.
