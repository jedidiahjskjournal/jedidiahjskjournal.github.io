The Three Pillars of an Unshaken Life

Three lessons I discovered in someone who never knew she was teaching me.

JJ

By Jedidiah Jsk

Jul 2026 • 8 min read

Not every teacher stands in a classroom. Some teach us simply by the way they live, often without ever knowing that someone is watching, learning, and changing because of them.

I met such a person someone the world may never know as a teacher, but whom I have come to regard as one of the greatest teachers of my life.

I found three rare qualities in her character: she was not ruled by what she did not have, not proud of what she had, and not imprisoned by what she had lost.

I first saw these qualities in her. Then I understood them. Then I learned from them. And eventually, I began making them part of my own life. (Take the good you find in people. Make it part of you.)

Some of the greatest teachers never know that they have taught us anything.

This article is my attempt to pass those lessons forward the three simple qualities that became three extraordinary pillars of an unshaken life.

1. The First Pillar - Do Not Be Ruled by What Is Missing

There will always be something we do not have. Someone will have more money, a better opportunity, a different life, or something we deeply wish were ours.

Wanting is not wrong. Dreams give us direction. But desire becomes dangerous when we tell ourselves, “I cannot be happy until I have this.” Then we are no longer pursuing something—we are allowing its absence to control us.

Want something deeply, but never hand it the keys to your happiness.

Work for what you want. Dream boldly. But do not postpone peace until life gives you everything you asked for. The horizon always moves; there will always be another version of “enough.”

The first pillar is simple: pursue what is missing, but do not become a prisoner of its absence.

2. The Second Pillar - Do Not Be Proud of What You Have

Success carries its own danger: pride. Money can become status, knowledge can become arrogance, beauty can become vanity, and achievement can slowly become identity.

Gratitude says, “I am fortunate to have this.” Pride says, “I am greater because I have this.”

A tree teaches something beautiful—the more fruit it carries, the lower its branches bend. Perhaps we should be the same. The more life gives us, the more humble we should become.

This reminds me of the spirit of Job: what is given can be received with gratitude, without assuming it must remain forever. Titles change. Wealth moves. Beauty fades. Applause finds someone else.

Enjoy what is in your hands. Use it well. Share it when you can. But never let what you possess become the measure of who you are.

Hold your achievements in your hands, never above your head.

3. The Third Pillar - Do Not Live Inside What You Lost

The hardest lesson may be our relationship with what is gone—a person, a friendship, an opportunity, money, status, a dream, or even an earlier version of ourselves.

Loss hurts, and acceptance does not mean pretending otherwise. But there is a difference between remembering something and allowing its absence to consume everything that still remains.

Memory is a place to visit, not a house in which to live.

A river never argues with the water that has already passed. It receives what comes next. Perhaps we must learn to do the same.

What left may have given us love, pain, wisdom—or all three. Take the lesson. Carry the gratitude. Leave the chains.

Three Pillars, Three Relationships With Time

Beneath these three lessons lies something deeper. What we do not have belongs to the future. What we have belongs to the present. What we lost belongs to the past.

We suffer when we become trapped in any of them—chasing the future, becoming proud of the present, or refusing to release the past.

The future asks for effort. The present asks for humility. The past asks for acceptance.

Perhaps life is best held with an open hand. An open hand can receive without arrogance, hold without fear, and let go when the time comes.

These principles are not against ambition. Build. Create. Earn. Love. Dream enormously. But keep your inner life independent of the scoreboard.

The Quiet Test

Think of something you want but do not have. Can you pursue it without allowing its absence to steal today's peace?

Think of something valuable you have. Can you enjoy it without believing it makes you greater than someone who does not have it?

Now think of something you have lost. Can you remember it without allowing yesterday to consume what remains today?

Character reveals itself in how we handle what we want, what we have, and what we lose.

Conclusion - An Unshaken Life

Life keeps moving things through our hands. Some things have not arrived yet. Some are with us now. Some have already left.

We cannot control all of it. But we can choose what power we give it.

Seek without desperation. Possess without arrogance. Lose without losing yourself.

Perhaps this is what my unknown teacher taught me without ever intending to teach me—not how to live without desire, success, or sorrow, but how to live without becoming their prisoner.

Do not measure your life only by what entered your hands. Measure it also by what never gained control of your heart.

Three pillars. What is missing should not rule you. What you have should not change you. What you lost should not destroy you.

Everything else will change. If these three pillars remain standing, perhaps we can remain standing too.
